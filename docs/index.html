What else could the new logo have bought?

In February 2024 Imperial replaced its “Imperial College London” logo with a new “Imperial” wordmark. According to its own Freedom of Information disclosure, the brand refresh cost £434,801.47. This page turns that money into beef, cows, free lunches, rent, PhD studentships, centrifuges, cell lines, culture medium, GPT tokens and new labs. Pick a budget, change the prices, and fill a basket of your own. Then see whether the brand has attracted more students, funding and partners, tested four ways and with Bayesian inference.

Did it work?

Has the new brand attracted more?

Imperial says the new brand will help it attract students, staff, funding and partners. More than two years after the launch on , we can start to check. We compare Imperial with the other 23 Russell Group universities before and after the launch. The comparison uses open data on undergraduate applicants (UCAS), research money from Wellcome, industry projects funded by Innovate UK, and Wikipedia page views as a measure of attention. Each outcome is estimated four ways, and a Bayesian model then asks how far the evidence should move what you already believed.

Four ways to estimate the effect

Each dot is one method's estimate of how much higher or lower Imperial's numbers are than they would have been without the new brand. The line is its 95% interval. If a line crosses zero, that method cannot tell the effect apart from no effect. Hover or tab to a dot for the details.

How the four methods work
Imperial's own trend
An interrupted time series. Fit a straight line (plus the usual monthly ups and downs) to Imperial before the launch, extend it, and measure how far Imperial strays from it afterwards. It ignores everything that happened to every other university at the same time.
Difference-in-differences
How much Imperial changed, minus how much the average comparison university changed. It assumes Imperial would otherwise have moved in step with that average.
Synthetic control
Build a "synthetic Imperial" from a weighted blend of comparison universities that tracked the real one before the launch, then compare the two afterwards. To judge the gap, every other university gets a placebo "rebrand" on the same date and the same test.
Bayesian synthetic control
The same idea as a Bayesian regression, in the spirit of Google's CausalImpact. It gives a full probability distribution for what Imperial would have done, shown as the shaded band below. Its intervals assume the regression is right, so they come out narrower than the placebo-based ones.

What the evidence does to a belief

Bayesian inference starts from a belief about the effect (the prior), weighs it against the data (the likelihood) and ends with an updated belief (the posterior). The data here are each outcome's synthetic-control gap, judged against the placebo gaps at the 23 other universities. Pick a starting belief. The verdicts at the top of this section change with it.

What it adds up to

Read with care

  • It is early. There is one UCAS cycle and one or two years of grants since the launch. Brands are meant to work over decades, and the last logo lasted 22 years.
  • Other things happened to Imperial too. Four months after the launch Imperial rose from sixth to second in the QS World University Rankings. Anything that changed for Imperial alone at the same time is mixed in with the brand's effect, so a positive result would be an upper limit.
  • These are proxies. Applicants are not students, Wellcome is one funder among many, Innovate UK projects are one kind of industry partnership, and page views measure attention, not attraction.
  • Staff are not tested. HESA's staff record is the only source that counts staff the same way at every university, and its website blocks automated downloads.
  • Recent years are still filling in. Innovate UK's 2025/26 list is still being updated, and the Russell Group's total is about half the year before. Every university is affected alike, but Imperial's small numbers that year are noisy.
  • Why trust the placebo test. With one rebranded university, the honest yardstick is how much the other universities drift anyway. Model-based intervals (Imperial's own trend, the Bayesian synthetic control) ignore that drift and can look more certain than they are.

Method

How the numbers are made

The bill. The total, £434,801.47, is Imperial’s own figure from its FOI disclosure log (March 2024), and Felix reported the same number. The College refused to give the amount paid to each supplier (FOI Act s.43). The split into agencies, typeface, signs and promotional material is therefore our estimate. We sized the signage bottom-up: identification signs, banners and lettering at the main campuses. The typeface and the promotional items are sized from typical commission costs, and the agencies get the remainder. Each slice has a range, and the sliders let you test it.

Today’s money. Amounts are re-priced with the ONS consumer price index (series D7BT): 2024 average ; 2002–03 average ; .

Conversions. Every card divides the budget by a unit price, and each card says where its price comes from. Most are UK list or average prices from 2026. The ultracentrifuge, the ATCC cell lines and the OpenAI tokens only have US dollar list prices, converted at the ECB rate of 30 October 2026 ($1 = £0.746). Lab equipment uses suppliers’ list prices, mostly before VAT. A few cards then turn the count into something easier to picture, using fixed assumptions listed in CALCULATIONS.md.

Code. A small Python model (src/logowhatif) reads the sourced inputs in data/. It writes this page’s data file, the step-by-step calculations and the results tables, and a test suite checks that the pieces add up. Data generated .

Fair’s fair

  • Money is not that interchangeable. The brand was paid for from central budgets, not research grants. A PhD studentship also needs a supervisor, a project and a desk, and a new lab needs people and running costs.
  • Imperial is big. The bill is about 0.03% of one year’s income, and the College says the brand will help it attract students, staff, funding and partners. Did it work? tests that claim.
  • Keeping the old logo was not free either. Signs wear out and fonts need licences. Imperial says the bespoke typeface cuts ongoing licensing fees.
  • The split is a guess; the total is not. Only Imperial knows what the signs cost. If you have a better figure, open an issue on GitHub.
  • Prices move. Each card says where and when its price was taken. Cows, beef and tokens in particular change quickly.
